======== Commitlog batch sweep: 64KB/1ms vs 1MB/5ms (2026-07-23) ========
Same cluster shape (3x perf-2x/4GB, ext4 volume, concurrent full-table scan load).
buffered-only ramp; 1MB run done MANUALLY against a healthy cluster after a
transient provisioning race killed the harness relaunch.

offered | DEFAULT 64KB/1ms          | 1MB/5ms batch
1000    |  997/s 100% p99=282ms     |  997/s 100% p99=281ms
2000    | 1990/s  99% p99=279ms     | 1993/s 100% p99=148ms
4000    | 3991/s 100% p99=88ms      | 3970/s  99% p99=82ms
8000    | 7961/s 100% p99=56ms      | 7956/s  99% p99=60ms
16000   | (csv not captured)        | 15065/s 94% p99=4268ms
32000   | 12861/s 40% p99=111363ms  | 12989/s 41% p99=109262ms

device write IOPS at the 16k stage: default ~9569/s vs 1MB ~8004/s.

FINDINGS:
- Clean range (1k-8k): identical (batching only matters near the knee).
- Batching is more I/O-EFFICIENT: fewer, fatter fsyncs -> fewer device IOPS
  (8004 vs 9569) for equal-or-more CQL throughput. Confirms the bottleneck in
  the moderate regime was fsync COUNT, not raw device IOPS (both < 17k ceiling).
- 1MB held 16k at 94% keep (15065/s) where the default had degraded -> extends
  the SUSTAINABLE range. (Default 16k CSV didn't capture; inferred from its
  8k-clean / 32k-collapsed trajectory, not a clean head-to-head.)
- BUT the ultimate overload floor is UNCHANGED: both collapse at 32k to ~13k/s,
  ~40% keep, ~110s latency. Under extreme overload the limiter shifts to
  compaction thrashing / memtable backpressure (memtable pinned at 208MB near
  the 256MB cap) -- which commitlog batching does not touch.

VERDICT: commitlog batching is a genuine but MODEST efficiency win (fewer IOPS,
more headroom before degradation) -- worth a moderate default bump -- but NOT a
ceiling-changer. The saturation floor (~13k) is compaction-bound. Next probe to
move the floor: compaction throughput / flush threshold, not batch size.
Reads-under-load still unmeasured (0 full-table scans completed in the window).
